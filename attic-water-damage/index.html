Attic Water Damage Help in South Gate

Attic water damage comes from two directions. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. Our team in South Gate inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

Signs to Look For

  • Spots of daylight at a chimney, a vent pipe, or a valley in the roof
  • Black or white fuzzy growth on the sheathing, often heaviest near the eaves
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Closets on the top floor, or the attic, that smell musty
  • A bath fan or ceiling light fixture with drips or a stain around it
  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Can Start

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

A few weeks on

Ceiling Stains Appear

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

After months

Rot Sets In

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Every day, a fan duct that stops short of the roof releases humid bathroom air into the attic. That moisture condenses on the cold sheathing and looks just like a roof leak.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

Air handlers, with their drain pans and condensate lines, sit in many attics. When the pan overflows or the drain clogs, insulation gets wet for weeks without any rain as a warning.

Wet Wiring

An attic contains recessed light cans, junction boxes and runs of cable. Do not enter the attic before someone checks the power, because wet electrical parts can shock you or start a fire.

Insulation That Stops Working

Once damp and packed down, insulation does little of its job and might not recover even when dry. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.
