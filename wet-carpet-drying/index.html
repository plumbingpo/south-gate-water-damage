Wet Carpet Drying: What South Gate Homeowners Should Know

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. In South Gate, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

Warning Signs

  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
  • The room smells sour and stale, especially after it has been closed for a while
  • Dark, rusty lines along the wall where the tack strip has gotten wet
  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Water Quality Drops

Water that started clean does not stay that way. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.

Furniture Marks

Furniture legs sitting on wet carpet release wood stain and rust into the fibers. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

Mold Under the Carpet

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Our Process

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reset

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Day 3 to 7

Carpet Starts Failing

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.
